Keepit: The amplified case for data sovereignty in the age of AI
Date and Time: November 11 at 11:30 a.m. (CET) Room: 117 Session code: SPS53
Data integrity is the precondition for trustworthy AI. When the data feeding your AI is incomplete or detached from its origin, the outputs carry that uncertainty. That integrity depends on sovereignty — the ability to access, recover, and verify your data independently. Leave this session with a practical framework for evaluating true recoverability, and a stronger sense of why sovereignty, governance, and AI strategy are inseparable.
You will leave this session with:
A clearer understanding of why data sovereignty is foundational to a trustworthy single point of truth
A framework for evaluating whether your organization can truly access and recover its data independently of the platforms it runs on
Practical insight into how SaaS environments quietly erode data control — and what the accountability gap looks like in practice

When the cloud goes dark — resilience lessons from the field
Date and Time: November 18 at 10:55–11:15 Location: Blue Stage (Blå Scene)
Hybrid threats don't announce themselves. They combine cyberattacks, infrastructure sabotage, and geopolitical pressure — and cloud services are a primary target. In this session, Morten Aagaard walks through real-world scenarios where SaaS availability fails and what that exposes for organizations built on hyperscaler infrastructure.
The session makes the case for keeping backup data outside the platforms it protects, in a location where data residency meets the regulatory requirements that apply to your organization. For European organizations, that means solutions built for European sovereignty requirements — not adapted from global hyperscaler stacks.
Attendees will leave with a clearer picture of where single-vendor dependencies create blind spots, why data sovereignty is a resilience issue as much as a compliance one, and how independent cloud backup changes the recovery equation when primary access is lost.
