- The number of records included in the restore

With these insights, you can:
- Reduce the risk of failed or partial restores

Google Workspace: Restore permissions alongside your Google Drive data
With permission backup and restore for Google Drive and shared drives, you can recover access settings alongside your content, helping you return your environment to a known state with greater confidence.
What's new
Keepit now backs up Google Workspace permissions for Google Drive and shared drives, enabling you to restore access settings together with your files. This enhancement allows you to:
- Restore who had access to specific files and folders at a given point in time.
- Recover permission levels alongside content during restore operations.
- Roll back unintended permission changes caused by cleanups, migrations, ownership changes, or automation errors.
- Maintain consistent access controls without relying on manual reconstruction from screenshots, logs, or memory.
By protecting permissions as part of your backup, Keepit helps preserve both your data and the access model that supports collaboration across your organization.
Why it matters
When permissions are lost or changed unexpectedly, productivity and security can be affected immediately. Restoring files without the correct access settings can leave users locked out of critical content or lead to overly broad sharing that increases risk. With permission-aware protection, you can:
- Restore access, not just files, so users can get back to work faster.
- Reduce the need for risky workarounds such as broad link sharing.
- Recover confidently after reorganizations, migrations, and large-scale changes.
- Support audit and compliance efforts by providing visibility into who had access to content at a specific point in time.

Azure DevOps: Work item relationship restore
Restore Azure DevOps Work Items with their relationships intact — including links between parent, child, related, and duplicate items. This update helps preserve project structure and context during recovery operations.
What’s new
When a Work Item is restored, Keepit now restores its connections to other Work Items alongside the item content itself.
This includes support for restoring relationships such as:
- Parent and child links.
- Related Work Items.
- Duplicate relationships.
- Other organizational dependencies between Work Items.
The restore process is designed to be non-destructive. Missing links are restored, while newer links created after the backup remain untouched.
This functionality also works for permanently deleted Work Items.
Why it matters
With this update, you get a more consistent and predictable recovery experience across deletion scenarios while preserving newer changes made after the backup. This helps maintain continuity, reduce manual reconstruction work, and protect the integrity of project relationships after recovery.

Entra ID: Switching to Graph V1 for logs backup (expected changes to snapshot sizes)
We’re introducing a change to how Entra ID sign-in logs are backed up in Keepit, ensuring more stable and reliable snapshots over time.
What's new
Keepit now uses the official Microsoft Graph API (v1.0) to collect Entra ID Interactive User Sign-In Logs. Previously backed-up logs via the Microsoft Graph Beta API included additional log types but were less stable for long-term use.
With this update:
- Interactive User Sign-In Logs continue to be backed up.
- Non-interactive User Sign-In Logs, Service Principal Sign-In Logs, and Managed Identity Sign-In Logs are no longer included in snapshots.
As a result, you may notice smaller snapshot growth over time, which is expected and reflects the updated log scope — not an issue with your data or environment.

This update ensures more stable and reliable snapshots, consistent backup behavior, and better long-term support from Microsoft.

Zendesk: Tagging restored tickets
Zendesk tickets created via a restore job are now automatically tagged.
What’s new
Zendesk tickets created through a restore job will now automatically receive a tag at the moment they are created. This tag is compatible with Zendesk’s automation framework, allowing admins to build rules that exclude restored tickets from specific automation actions.
When starting a restore, you can define the tag to be applied. You can also identify which tickets were created through a restore both directly in the Zendesk interface and in Keepit.
Why it matters
Zendesk does not natively distinguish restored tickets from user-submitted ones. As a result, restored tickets are treated as newly created and can trigger default automations and notifications, including messages sent to external customers.
By flagging restored tickets, admins can configure workflows to handle restores differently. This helps prevent unintended customer notifications and reduces disruption to SLAs, reporting, and internal processes.

We now support images embedded in Salesforce Rich Text Fields, including JPEG, PNG, PJPEG, and GIF formats. These images are stored as Salesforce Attachments and retrieved via the REST API.
On your next full scan, any embedded images will be detected and stored. Because Attachments are not Bulk API-compatible, this may result in slower performance for that initial scan — especially for orgs with a high volume of embedded images. After this one-time sync, backup performance should return to normal unless large volumes of changes continue to occur in rich text fields.
