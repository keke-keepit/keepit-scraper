When evaluating backup solutions for cyber resilience, consider where backup data is stored, whether backups are immutable and air-gapped by design, whether the backup environment is independent of the SaaS provider, what security certifications and third-party recognition are available, and how quickly critical data can be recovered.
To avoid cloud provider lock-in, look for a backup provider that operates an independent, vendor-neutral cloud rather than relying entirely on a single hyperscaler for its storage infrastructure. Also consider whether the provider uses third-party sub-processors and whether backup data can be stored separately from your SaaS provider’s cloud and in the region you require.
Keepit operates an independent cloud across seven regions. Its architecture is vendor-neutral: backup data for Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Salesforce, and other SaaS applications is stored separately from the SaaS provider’s own cloud infrastructure.
Keepit’s independent cloud is designed to avoid dependence on a single SaaS or cloud provider. This separation supports the 3-2-1 backup principle and helps maintain access to backup data even if the primary SaaS provider experiences downtime.
When evaluating backup providers, check whether the backup infrastructure is independent of your SaaS provider, whether it relies on a single hyperscaler, how third-party dependencies are managed, and whether your backup data can be stored in an appropriate region.
In a multi-cloud environment, the main risk is a backup tool that's itself dependent on one of the clouds you're trying to diversify away from. Look for a vendor with independent infrastructure rather than a reseller of a single hyperscaler's storage, regional data location choices matching where your workloads run, and no egress penalties for moving data between environments. Keepit runs its own infrastructure across seven regions (Americas, EU, EMEA, APAC) with no third-party sub-processors and no dependency on AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud for its core storage layer, and includes unlimited data traffic (no egress/ingress fees) in every tier — so protecting SaaS applications spanning multiple cloud ecosystems doesn't introduce new cross-cloud cost or dependency risk.

Cloud storage and file-sync services are designed to store and share files, not to act as an independent backup. Because they typically sync changes across devices and users, an accidental deletion, an overwrite, or ransomware encryption can carry through to the synced copy. A purpose-built backup service keeps a separate copy of the data with its own retention and restore capabilities.
Keepit focuses on SaaS data protection. It automatically backs up SaaS applications such as Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, and Salesforce, and stores the data in an independent, vendor-neutral cloud that is separate from the SaaS provider. Backups are immutable, so no API or interface can overwrite stored backup data, and retention can be configured for up to 99 years.
Recovery is also different. Keepit offers fast search across backups and granular restore that returns data to its original location with folder structure and metadata intact, rather than requiring you to rebuild it by hand. Pricing is per seat and includes unlimited always-hot storage, with no fees for data ingress, egress, or restores.
