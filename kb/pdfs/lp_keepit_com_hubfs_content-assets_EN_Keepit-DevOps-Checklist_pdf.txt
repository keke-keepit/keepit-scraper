Introduction
Your development stack is more than tools. It’s your product roadmap, your intellectual property, your 
velocity, and your institutional knowledge. Platforms like Jira, Confluence, and GitHub sit at the center 
of how modern teams build and ship software. 
 
But two recent realities are forcing a rethink: 
•	
The shift away from on-premises support by Atlassian is accelerating cloud dependence 
•	
Even leading platforms can experience data integrity issues, including incidents impacting pull/
merge requests 
And here’s the uncomfortable truth: If your collaboration and development data is lost, corrupted, or 
overwritten, most native protections won’t bring it back the way you need. 
 
Here’s a quick checklist to help you quickly assess your exposure: 
1. Do you have source code and repository protection?
Organizations that have built strong safeguards around their development data can 
recover deleted repositories, branches, and commits to a precise point in time, supported 
by independent backups. They can also restore pull requests, merge history, and commit 
metadata, preserving the full context of development activity. Protections against accidental 
or automated deletions further reduce risk, ensuring code integrity and continuity across the 
software lifecycle. 
 
Without these capabilities, development environments become highly vulnerable. Lost re-
positories or corrupted history are difficult—if not impossible—to fully reconstruct, especially 
without external backups or metadata. Even small deletions can cascade into broken builds 
and stalled workflows. Left unchecked, this risk leads to permanent loss of code history, 
unrecoverable development work, and significant disruption to engineering productivity.
2. Do you have work management and ticketing resilience?
Organizations that have strong control over their work management data and processes 
can recover issues, tickets, and workflows, along with historical sprint data and backlog 
prioritization. Automation rules and custom workflows can be restored, and version history 
provides visibility into changes over time. This ensures continuity across planning, execution, 
and delivery, even in the face of disruption. 
 
Without these capabilities, operational visibility and coordination quickly break down. Lost 
tickets, missing sprint data, or broken workflows disrupt planning and execution, while the 
lack of version history makes it difficult to understand or fix issues. Left unchecked, this 
results in loss of product planning, delivery tracking, and overall operational continuity.

3. Do you have knowledge and documentation continuity?
Organizations that have strong safeguards in place for preserving institutional knowledge 
can restore pages, spaces, and attachments, with historical versions fully recoverable to 
maintain accuracy and context. Critical runbooks and internal documentation are backed up 
independently, and permissions and access structures can be restored, ensuring the right 
people retain access to the right information. This supports smooth onboarding, consistent 
operations, and continuity of knowledge across teams. 
 
Without these capabilities, knowledge loss becomes a real risk. Missing documentation, lost 
version history, or broken permissions create confusion and slow teams down. Onboarding 
becomes more difficult without reliable resources, and day-to-day operations are impacted 
by gaps in information. Left unchecked, this results in loss of institutional knowledge, 
increased friction, and operational delays.
4. Do you have ransomware and insider threat protection?
Organizations that have built strong protections for collaboration and development data 
integrity ensure code, tickets, and documentation are stored immutably, so they cannot 
be altered or deleted, and backups are isolated from production environments to prevent 
attackers from compromising both at once. Teams can roll back malicious or unauthorized 
changes and rely on tested recovery scenarios for compromised developer accounts. This 
enables a clean, controlled recovery that preserves trust in development systems. 
 
Without these protections, the risk is severe. Attackers or unauthorized changes can corrupt 
code, tickets, and documentation—and potentially impact backups—eliminating any reliable 
recovery point. Without the ability to roll back changes or execute a proven recovery process, 
issues can spread quickly across systems. Left unchecked, this results in widespread data 
corruption with no clean path to recovery, forcing prolonged disruption and complex rebuild 
efforts. 
5. Do you have operational recovery capabilities?
Organizations that have built a recovery model that supports both speed and precision 
across their collaboration and development environment can perform full environment 
restores across tools while also executing fast, granular recovery of repositories, tickets, or 
documentation. Recovery time objectives (RTOs) are clearly defined and achievable, and 
the process does not rely on manual exports or scripts. This ensures consistent, predictable 
recovery and keeps engineering teams productive even during disruptions. 
 
Without these capabilities, recovery becomes slow and error-prone. Limited restore options, 
unclear RTOs, and reliance on manual processes introduce delays and inconsistencies. Even 
small issues can escalate into broader outages that stall development work. Left unchecked, 
this results in prolonged downtime and significant loss of engineering productivity.

6. Do you have platform dependency and change risk?
Organizations that have reduced their dependence on vendors for data protection and 
recovery don’t rely solely on native retention policies and are prepared for platform-level 
changes, such as shifts to cloud-only models. With the ability to recover data independently 
of vendor availability and clear contingency plans for service disruptions, they maintain 
control and continuity regardless of external changes. 
 
Without these capabilities, organizations are tightly coupled to vendor performance and 
decisions. Changes to platforms, outages, or limited retention policies can quickly disrupt 
access to critical data. If recovery depends on the vendor, options become limited during 
incidents. Left unchecked, this risk results in vendor-driven disruptions that directly impact 
your ability to operate.
7. Do you have compliance, audit and IP protection?
Organizations that have strong governance and protection in place for their development and 
operational data maintain audit trails of changes across repositories and tickets, can support 
legal hold and forensic investigations, and ensure backup data aligns with compliance 
requirements. Intellectual property is further protected through independent recovery, giving 
teams the ability to restore critical assets without relying on a single system. This creates 
defensibility, traceability, and confidence in how data is managed and protected. 
 
Without these capabilities, organizations face significant legal and compliance exposure. 
Limited auditability, inability to support investigations, and gaps in backup coverage 
make it difficult to prove control over data. Intellectual property becomes vulnerable to 
loss or compromise without a reliable recovery path. Left unchecked, this risk can lead to 
compliance failures, legal challenges, and increased exposure of critical assets.
