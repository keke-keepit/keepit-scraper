For years, the backup platform protecting Google Workspace at Point Loma Nazarene 
University sat mostly unused. Joe Alcorn, director of systems and DevOps, says his team 
restored from it only once, because it was too confusing to trust. Point Loma wanted a 
backup and recovery platform its team would use, backed by a vendor that stayed engaged 
long after the deal closed. Ten months into working with Keepit, that trust has helped Alcorn 
close out a project that sat untouched for a decade.€

Legacy platform nobody wanted to use
Before Keepit, Alcorn's team managed backup for 
around 1,500 staff and faculty accounts on a 
platform he calls "funky." Finding a file meant 
knowing its exact original path. There was no way 
to search by shared drive. It also wasn't clear 
whether a settings change applied to already-
backed-up users, or if it required removing and(
re-adding them. The team restored data from it 
exactly once in years, and Point Loma's former CIO 
treated it as a checkbox rather than real 
protection.

A different kind of vendor relationship 
Alcorn first ran into Keepit at an EDUCAUSE 
conference, where he talked with the team at the 
booth and later worked through a proof of 
concept. What stood out wasn't just the platform. 
It was the engagement. The previous vendor 
mostly surfaced at renewal time, but Keepit 
reached out proactively to keep onboarding and 
setup on track. 

Anomaly detection, audit logs, and self-
service links 
Within the first few months, Alcorn stopped 
worrying about whether he could find what he
needed. It was all there, and easy to find quickly. 
That confidence grew further after Point Loma  
expanded its Keepit licensing: Keepit's monitoring 
flagged the resulting data increase as anomalous 
and checked in. The jump made sense once Alcorn 
traced it to 2,000 newly added users, but the 
outreach itself told him something was watching 
the data closely. Ask Alcorn which features he likes 
most, and two come up fast: audit logs and the 
self-service restore link that lets end users search 
for and recover their own files through a secure 
link.
