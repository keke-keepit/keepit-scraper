The anatomy of recovery: Webinar key takeaways on intelligent disaster recovery
Disaster recovery (DR) is more than just having a plan — it’s about ensuring that plan actually works when it matters most. Without risk assessments, regular testing, and clear recovery priorities, businesses leave themselves vulnerable to unexpected disruptions. These challenges were the focus of a recent Keepit webinar, where Kim Larsen, CISO at Keepit, joined Paul Robichaux, Microsoft MVP and Senior Director of Product at Keepit, and Martin Husslage, Disaster Recovery Consultant at Zerto, to discuss real-world data loss scenarios and best practices for building an intelligent DR strategy.
Here’s what we learned:
1) Testing is critical to disaster recovery success.
Merely having a DR plan isn’t enough — you need to validate it. Organizations must regularly test their DR plans to ensure their recovery strategies hold up under real-world conditions and can mitigate potential disruptions effectively.
2) Backup redundancy ensures business continuity.
To safeguard against cyberthreats and operational failures, businesses must implement immutable backups stored separately from the production environment. Ensuring air-gapped storage and redundancy strengthens overall resilience and minimizes downtime.
3) Intelligent recovery planning prevents costly failures.
A well-structured DR plan prioritizes business-critical systems and defines a clear recovery order to minimize the impact of disruptions. Aligning with industry best practices ensures a faster, more efficient recovery process when disaster strikes.

Are you breach ready? Key takeaways on how immutability minimizes damage
In today’s threat landscape, breaches don’t unfold slowly — they strike in minutes. Attackers routinely bypass perimeter defenses, exploit identity weaknesses, and attempt to destroy backups to maximize damage. The question is no longer if you’ll be targeted, but whether your organization is prepared to recover when it happens.
This was the focus of a recent Keepit webinar, where Paul Robichaux, Senior Director of Product Management at Keepit and Microsoft MVP, and Bart Binder, Red Team Manager at Keepit, explored why immutability and isolation have become essential pillars of modern cyber resilience.
Here’s what we learned:
- Immutability isn’t optional — it’s the foundation of recovery. 
 Attackers often try to purge logs and mass-delete backups. In one example discussed during the webinar, the only reason the attack chain was broken was because the storage layer enforced true WORM at ingest — preventing any modification or deletion attempts. When everything else fails, immutability becomes your last line of defense.
- Modern breaches move fast — your defenses must be faster. 
 Kill chains unfold in minutes. Immutability, anchored time, and no-modify APIs give your team the critical time needed to respond before damage becomes irreversible.
- Isolation matters just as much as immutability. 
 Air-gapping has evolved. Storing Microsoft 365 data in a vendor-independent cloud — without shared credentials, infrastructure, or replication paths — removes a major attack surface. If an attacker can reach every replica, it’s not real protection.
Our webinar handout summarizes these insights into actionable guidance, including:
- What “true immutability” means — and which designs fall short
- How isolation, short-lived tokens, and separated control/storage planes improve protection
- Breach-ready actions your team should implement now
Download the handout now to learn how to turn principles into practice — and ensure your organization is truly breach-ready. In a world where attackers only need minutes, your recovery strategy is your strongest defense.

From threat to defense: Webinar key takeaways on ransomware and cyber insurance
Ransomware plagues security professionals — and, in turn, the companies that insure them — more than ever before. It has now become one of the most pressing cybersecurity challenges, with 89% of enterprises identifying it as a top-five threat to their business. This threat was the focus of a recent webinar, where Kim Larsen, CISO at Keepit, and a panel of cybersecurity experts discussed the evolving role of cyber insurance as part of a risk mitigation strategy.
Here’s what we learned:
1) Proactive defense is critical.
To counter ransomware threats, organizations must focus on securing initial access points, conducting regular vulnerability assessments, and implementing compartmentalization to prevent lateral movement.
2) Backup integrity ensures operational continuity. 
Maintaining offsite backups that are kept physically and logically separate from the production environment, frequently checking for corruption, and routine recovery exercises are essential steps for safeguarding critical data and ensuring backup integrity.
3) Cyber insurance can be a cornerstone of risk management. 
Cyber insurance complements proactive defense measures, offering a financial safety net against sophisticated threats. However, securing coverage now demands rigorous proof of cybersecurity maturity as insurers increasingly demand proof of implementation for cyber insurability controls.

Data governance is no longer a back-office task — it’s a strategic pillar of cyber resilience. Modern recovery plans must not only aim for rapid response but also ensure the completeness of recovered data — all while managing cost-effectiveness. Organizations need to prioritize business-critical data and services to ensure continuity during a disruption.

When disaster strikes: A guide to data recovery and business continuity
This 7-step guide to data recovery and business continuity offers IT professionals, DR pros, and CIOs insight into the steps of a disaster recovery process for SaaS apps.
Every day, businesses shift critical operations into cloud-based, software-as-a-service (SaaS) applications. At the same time, cybercriminals are directing more effort toward cloud services, putting businesses at risk.
Most businesses have some form of continuity and disaster recovery (DR) plan, but these plans often overlook crucial SaaS app data needed to maintain or recover operations — thereby representing an essential gap in organizations’ preparedness levels. And even if SaaS data is backed up and protected, disaster recovery plans are often not continually tested and therefore can’t be relied on in the face of emergency.
In the pre-SaaS paradigm, organizations could typically perform a full disaster recovery on their own, but the move to the cloud has introduced new complications and dependencies:
- Full SaaS application restoration requires the tenant to be restored and repopulated with data.
- Maintaining continuity in the meantime requires accessing SaaS data.
This guide discusses how disaster recovery planning and DR objectives need to change to adapt to the new requirements of an agile, cloud-integrated business. Keepit's Take on The Forrester New Wave™: SaaS Application Data Protection, Q4 2021
The adoption of SaaS applications was widely enhanced through the global pandemic of Covid-19 as businesses around the world had to enable their employees to work and collaborate digitally. While SaaS providers (such as Microsoft, Google, and Salesforce) focus on their platform uptime, their clients are left alone with their data resiliency and durability needs. - Critical SaaS data applications: 50% of respondents have included data cloud-stored in critical SaaS applications in their disaster recovery plans, and 40% plan to do so.

The compliance labyrinth: Webinar key takeaways
Global cybersecurity regulations are evolving rapidly to address the growing threat of cybercrime. Simultaneously, the current, fragmented regulatory landscape creates challenges for organizations striving to achieve compliance. In our recent webinar, Kim Larsen, CISO at Keepit, and a panel of industry experts explored the challenges and opportunities presented by global cybersecurity regulations.
Here’s what we learned:
- Cybersecurity regulations are evolving.
Jimmy Nilson, Global VP at Kyndryl Security Consulting: “A common trend in cybersecurity regulations is that all of them are changing. All of them are being updated because the environment we operate in today looks vastly different than only a couple of years ago. That’s because we have a much bigger attack surface, and we introduced more advanced technology to support all our organizations.”   
- Common cybersecurity principles are emerging.
Widely adopted frameworks such as NIST and ISO offer commonalities for orientation. 
- Risk assessment and data governance are crucial.
Effective risk assessment and solid data governance are key foundational building blocks to achieve operational resilience.

Your vendor evaluation checklist: SaaS backup and recovery
Evaluation checklist to simplify your vendor selection
Choosing the right SaaS backup and recovery vendor shouldn’t be a guessing game. When downtime, data loss, or ransomware strike, you need partners that can deliver fast, reliable recovery — not vague promises. That’s why we built this vendor evaluation checklist.
It’s designed to help you evaluate vendors consistently and objectively. The questions map to the managed stage of our disaster recovery maturity framework — where DR is integrated with security, compliance, communications, and leadership decision-making. Organizations at this level don’t just react to incidents — they anticipate them.
By using this checklist, you’ll get deeper insight into which vendors can truly support your SaaS resilience strategy and help you move toward proactive, business-aligned recovery.
What questions and topics are part of the checklist?
Below are the core sections included in the vendor evaluation checklist. Each one walks you through the right questions to ask — and why it matters for achieving mature SaaS resilience: 
- Backup coverage and data protection
- Backup and restore features
- Storage and retention
- Security and compliance
- Monitoring, reporting, and observability
- Vendor capabilities and reputation
Use these sections to guide vendor conversations and ensure every evaluation is consistent, measurable, and aligned with your organization’s expectations for resilience.

To make this process easier and to simplify your vendor evaluation, we’ve created a downloadable template you can use to score vendors on these different questions. Get the RFI template now to simplify your vendor evaluation.
